04The nine rules that decide your night

Everything is published before registration opens, so you can read exactly how you’ll be judged and then decide.

  1. Teams of 2 to 4, and everyone signs for themselvesBring your own complete team — there is no team-matching pool. Every team is scored the same way regardless of size. The Team Leader submits the team’s verification video; each member accepts the terms with their own email. The leader cannot accept on anyone else’s behalf.
  2. All AI tools are allowedClaude, ChatGPT, Cursor, Copilot, Gemini — anything. No limits and nothing to declare. How well you direct them is part of what’s being looked at.
  3. Your setup is your responsibilityInternet, electricity, laptop, camera, microphone. You’re asked for a backup plan at registration because you’re expected to have one: a second connection, a charged power bank, everything tested before the day.
  4. A dead connection does not stop the clockIf you drop, the four hours keep running. No restart, no extra time, no re-run. Post in your team channel when you’re back so there’s a record. The only exception is an area-wide outage taking your whole team offline at once, judged by the event lead on the night.
  5. Camera on, and a proctor is in the roomA proctor sits in your room for the full four hours, with four formal checks announced in your channel beforehand. Failing to appear within 3 minutes of a formal check is the violation. Blurred or virtual backgrounds are completely fine.
  6. Everything runs through your team channelOnce confirmed you get a private Discord channel with your proctor in it. Camera checks, outage reports, questions and submission links all go there. The server is for confirmed teams only.
  7. Host it anywhere, but it must open at 10:00 PMVercel, Netlify, Render, Cloudflare, your own server. Every URL is health-checked and screenshotted once at the close, and that capture is what gets judged. A free instance asleep at 21:55, or a deploy you broke at 21:58, is on you.
  8. Your speed bonus comes from your last commitKeep building and your judged score goes up while the speed bonus comes down. Stop early and you keep the bonus. The bonus is zero unless 3 of the 4 required MVP bullets work on both problems.
  9. Open source is fine, with two limitsNo AGPL, GPL, LGPL, MPL or SSPL, and nothing marked non-commercial. MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD and ISC are all fine. You ship a short LICENSES.md listing what you used.

Allowed vs. not allowed

Allowed, without restriction

  • AI coding tools — all of them
  • Any language, framework, library or package
  • Open-source code, templates, UI kits, component libraries, starter kits (subject to licence limits)
  • Public docs, Stack Overflow, tutorials, your own past public repositories
  • Any hosting provider, design tool, stock asset or icon set

Not allowed

  • Any code written for these specific problems before 18:00 on 30 August
  • Help from anyone who is not a registered member of your team
  • Sharing problems, solutions or approaches with another team during the event
  • AGPL / GPL / LGPL / MPL / SSPL or any other copyleft component
  • Any asset under a non-commercial or personal-use-only licence
  • Code owned by your employer or a client without the right to license it commercially

The line between AI tools and outside help: an AI tool is a tool, a person is a person. Prompt Claude for an entire component if you like. Do not text a friend to write one.

Prayer, breaks and outages

Maghrib and Isha both fall inside the build window. A prayer break is granted on request, at any point in the four hours — post in your team channel or tell your proctor, then go. There’s no fixed window and nothing to schedule. Your camera does not have to stay on, a formal check landing inside a requested break is not a violation, and no screenshot from it is used.

Separately, every team’s early-submission clock is reduced by a flat 20 minutes automatically, applied identically whether a team prays or not. The credit isn’t tied to asking for a break, and asking doesn’t increase it.

05How the 100 marks work

Six categories. Two are automatic, four are judged. Categories 1, 4, 5 and 6 are scored once per problem and averaged across your two problems.

#CategoryMarksSource
1Functionality25Judged
2Early submission10Automatic
3Problem difficulty15Automatic
4Technical execution15Judged
5UI / UX15Judged
6Demo and documentation20Judged
Total100

The two automatic categories are 25 of 100 marks, and both are tied to work that actually functions. Difficulty and speed are multipliers on real work, not substitutes for it.

1 · Functionality — 25 marks

The heaviest category. Every problem lists 4 required MVP bullets. They are pass or fail, and a judge checks each in under a minute.

BandMarksDescriptor
Exceptional23–25Every MVP bullet works. Edge cases handled without being asked. The build shows the team understood the real pain in the scenario, not just the bullet list.
Strong18–22Every MVP bullet works. Happy path solid, edges thin.
Adequate13–17Most bullets work. One missing or partial. Demoable.
Weak7–12Runs, but half the requirements are missing or the core flow breaks under real input.
Minimal1–6Runs, but does not meaningfully address the problem.
Non-functional0Does not run.

2 · Early submission — 10 marks

1.25 marks for every complete 30 minutes remaining when you stop working, measured from your last commit across both repositories — not from a form. Partial blocks don’t count: a last commit at 2h 15m gives 3 blocks (3.75), not 3.5.

Last commit atRemainingBlocksBonus
3h 30m30m11.25
3h 00m1h 00m22.50
2h 30m1h 30m33.75
2h 00m2h 00m45.00
1h 30m2h 30m56.25
1h 00m3h 00m67.50
0h 30m3h 30m78.75 — practical max

The gate. The bonus is awarded only if at least 3 of the 4 required MVP bullets are verified working on each of your two problems. Below that threshold the bonus is 0 no matter how early you stopped. Without the gate, one strategy dominates: pick two Hard problems, ship broken skeletons in the first hour, and beat teams that spent four hours building working software.

3 · Problem difficulty — 15 marks

Credit = tier value × (required MVP bullets working ÷ total required bullets for that problem). Two problems, so the ceiling is 15.0 (two Hard).

Tier values

TierFull credit
Easy5.0
Medium6.5
Hard7.5

Worked examples

ExampleCalculationCredit
Hard, 4/4 bullets7.5 × 1.007.5
Hard, 3/4 bullets7.5 × 0.755.6
Easy, 4/4 bullets5.0 × 1.005.0
Hard, does not run7.5 × 00

Picking Hard and shipping nothing is worth less than picking Easy and shipping something that works.

4 · Technical execution — 15 marks

BandMarksDescriptor
Exceptional14–15Clean data model reflecting the domain. Sensible separation of concerns. Deployed properly. Real edge-case handling.
Strong11–13Reasonable structure, readable code, clean deployment.
Adequate7–10Works, but structure is ad hoc and state is handled loosely.
Weak3–6Copy-paste heavy, no discernible model, barely deployed.
Minimal0–2Unrunnable outside the authors’ machines.

AI tool use is not penalised. A well-structured AI-assisted build scores higher than a badly structured hand-written one. Commit history is an integrity signal, not a scoring dimension.

5 · UI / UX — 15 marks

BandMarksDescriptor
Exceptional13–15A stranger can use it without instructions. Layout serves the task. Empty, loading and error states handled.
Strong10–12Clean and usable, one or two rough states.
Adequate7–9Functional and plain. Default components, no thought applied.
Weak3–6Confusing layout, unlabelled controls, broken on narrow screens.
Minimal0–2Unstyled output.

Not scored: decoration for its own sake. A plain clear interface beats a decorated confusing one.

6 · Demo and documentation — 20 marks

ComponentMarks
Demo video, no less than 60 seconds, shows it working, covers the MVP bullets8
README — what it does, how to run it, what is mocked, what is next6
LICENSES.md, complete and accurate4
Submission completeness — repo, live URL, picks declared, fields correct2

The video scores above 4 only if a judge can verify claimed MVP bullets in the recording, and those bullets are then confirmed live. A polished video of a product that does not do what it shows scores nothing.

Tiebreaks, in order

  1. Higher Functionality score
  2. Higher Functionality on the team’s better-scoring problem
  3. Earlier last commit
  4. Higher Technical execution
  5. Event lead’s decision, recorded with reasons

How judging runs

StageWhat happens
Auto-scoreDifficulty and bonus computed from the archive. No human input.
ScreenDoes the URL load, does it address the declared problem. Pass or fail, with a one-line written reason for every fail, confirmed by the Judging Lead before it is actioned.
Deep judgeFunctionality, Technical execution, UI/UX, Demo scored against the bands above.
CalibrationBefore live scoring, all judges independently score the same two anchor submissions and reconcile.
Double-scoringEvery submission within 5 marks of a prize boundary is scored by two judges and averaged.
Sign-offFinal table, tiebreaks, appeals.

If the submitted code does not address the problem it was declared under, that problem’s difficulty credit is 0 — not just its Functionality score.

06What a complete submission looks like

Five things, for each of your two problems. Miss one and the submission is incomplete.

  • Source code submitted through the provided submission link. Submit as you build; make sure it’s uploaded before the deadline.
  • A live URL that loads for a judge who has never seen your project, with no setup steps. Host it however you like.
  • A README — what it does, how to run it, what is mocked, what you would build next.
  • A demo video, no less than 60 seconds. Show it working. Do not explain your architecture.
  • LICENSES.md listing every third-party dependency, template or asset with its licence.

Declare your two picks in the pick form as soon as you decide — that’s separate from your submission and starts no clock.

The archive you are judged against

At 22:00 on event day, before anything is judged: every repository’s write access is revoked and its commit and push history captured, and every live URL gets an automated HTTP check plus a full-page screenshot, archived. Judging happens a week later against that archive — so a free-tier container that falls asleep on 3 September cannot cost you marks for work that was live at 22:00 on 30 August.

07Ownership — read this one twice

Section 9 of the Rulebook. The organisers themselves flag it as the clause to be certain about before registering.

You assign to LofiStack all rights in the work you submit — source code, assets, designs and documentation, for both problems. LofiStack may use, modify, publish, commercialise or build on any submission without further permission or payment.

How it’s accepted

Every member accepts individually. After registering, each member receives their own acceptance link with the full clause and a signature field. The team is not confirmed until all members have accepted, and a team leader cannot accept on anyone else’s behalf.

What you keep

  • The right to show your submission in your portfolio and say publicly that you built it
  • Ownership of pre-existing code and open-source components you brought in, under their original licences
  • Your name on your work — credited wherever a submission is used or published

You also warrant that you own or have the right to assign everything you submit, and agree to indemnify LofiStack if a third party later claims rights in it. For participants under 18, that warranty and indemnity are given by the guardian who signs the consent form.

The organisers’ own wording: “if you build something here and we later turn it into a product, that is ours to do. If that is not acceptable to you, please do not register.”

08Internships — how they’re actually decided

Up to three individuals are offered a three-month internship at LofiStack. Selection is individual, not team-based — a strong individual on a team that placed nowhere is fully eligible.

Proctors score every participant 1 to 5 on six signals, recorded live at the 19:30, 20:30 and 21:30 marks rather than reconstructed afterwards.

SignalWhat it looks like
DecompositionPlanned before typing
CommunicationExplains thinking clearly, asks good questions
Debugging under pressureStays methodical when something breaks at hour three
OwnershipTakes a piece and finishes it
Tool judgementDirects AI tools and checks their output rather than pasting blindly
ComposureBehaviour when things go wrong, and toward teammates

Month 1 is a structured training period. Months 2 and 3 are paid, subject to performance. Full terms come with the offer letter. Participants under 18 are not eligible for internship offers.

The organisers say it plainly: these four hours are also an interview, and they think that beats a CV screen.

  • A screenshot is taken at each of the four announced camera checks (~18:30, 19:30, 20:30, 21:30)

10Code of conduct

“Treat everyone as a colleague you will see again.” That is the whole rule — everything else is detail. It applies in every event space, to participants, proctors, judges, moderators and organisers alike.

Expected

  • Assume good faith — people are building under time pressure on unfamiliar problems
  • Ask questions publicly in the Discord server so answers stay fair for everyone
  • Credit open-source work you use
  • Respect the proctors and your own teammates

Not tolerated

  • Harassment of any kind — gender, religion, ethnicity, age, disability, appearance or background
  • Sexual language or imagery in any event space
  • Personal attacks, insults, public humiliation of a participant or team
  • Sharing anyone’s personal information without consent, including screenshots of private messages
  • Deliberate disruption of another team’s work, room or connection
  • Sustained aggression toward staff, judges or moderators — including after the event, over results
SituationConsequence
Minor, first timePrivate warning
Repeated, or ignored a warningRemoval from the Discord server, event participation reviewed
SeriousImmediate disqualification, forfeiture of any prize, removal from all channels
SevereThe above, plus permanent exclusion from all future LofiStack and AskTechGuy events

A team is not disqualified for one member’s conduct unless the team knew and did nothing. Concerns about a proctor, judge or organiser go to the reporting address below, which deliberately does not reach the proctors or the event lead.

12Questions, answered

The things people ask most.

Is it really free?
Yes. No entry fee, no hidden charge, nothing to pay at any point.
Do I need exactly three people?
No. Two, three or four. Every team is scored the same way whatever its size.
I don’t have a team yet — can you find me one?
No. There is no team-matching pool. You arrive with your own team of two to four and register as a complete team. The Discord server only opens to teams once they are confirmed.
Can I use AI tools?
Yes, all of them, with no limits and nothing to declare. It’s how the work is done now, and they would rather watch you do it well.
Can I ask questions before the day?
Yes — live Q&A on Discord, Saturday 29 August at 4:00 PM. Voice, about 45 minutes, optional. Anything except the problems, which nobody sees before 6:00 PM on the 30th. Outside that, ask in the Discord server rather than by DM so answers stay fair.
What if my internet dies during the event?
The clock keeps running — no restart, no extra time, no re-run. Post in your team channel from your phone when you’re back so there’s a record; being off camera during a reported outage is not itself a violation. The one exception is an area-wide outage taking your whole team offline at once, judged by the event lead on the night.
Do I really have to keep my camera on for four hours?
Yes. A proctor is in your room for the whole window, with four formal checks announced beforehand. Blurred or virtual backgrounds are completely fine, and your microphone stays muted unless you’re talking to your proctor. The proctor’s notes are also what internship offers are based on.
Where do I host my project?
Anywhere — free plans are fine. All that’s needed is a URL that opens for someone who has never seen your project, and it has to be up at 10:00 PM, because that’s when everything is health-checked and screenshotted. What’s captured then is what gets judged.
How is it scored?
100 marks across six areas. The full rubric is published before registration opens, so you can read exactly how you’ll be judged before deciding to enter.
When do I find out if I won?
Around 6 September, one week after the event. The extra week is deliberate, so every submission gets read properly. Every team that submits gets its own score breakdown with the judges’ notes.
Who owns what I build?
LofiStack. Section 9 of the Rulebook has it in full — you have to open that document and mark it read before the registration form appears, and every member then accepts it individually with their own email.
Can I still get an internship offer if my team doesn’t win?
Yes. Offers go to individuals, based on what proctors saw during the event, not on where your team finished.
Can I enter if I’m under 18?
Yes, from age 16, with a guardian consent form — one page, downloadable from the event site. Under-18 participants aren’t eligible for internship offers, and prize money is paid into a guardian’s account.
What happens after I register?
You first receive a registration confirmation email after submitting your application. If your team is selected, you then receive a verification confirmation email with the next steps to complete team verification.

13How registration works

About four minutes, once you have everyone’s details in front of you. Registration closed Thursday 27 August, 23:59 BST.

  1. Read the four documents. Rulebook, Scoring Rubric, Code of Conduct, and Recording & Data Notice. The registration form stays locked until all four are opened and marked read — roughly ten minutes.
  2. Fill in your team. Team leader registers the whole group and becomes the single point of contact.
  3. Each member accepts the terms individually via their own email link, including the ownership clause. The leader cannot accept for anyone else.
  4. Team leader submits a 30-second verification video holding photo ID, plus every member’s GitHub username.
  5. Under 18? Guardian signs the consent form; the leader uploads it as a PDF with the team’s verification.

Registration is uncapped, but places on event day are limited by proctor capacity. If verified teams exceed capacity, places are allocated in order of registration completion.
