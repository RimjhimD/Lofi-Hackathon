Problem 7 — Relief Lens (Tier: Hard)

Category: Civic / crisis response
Stack: any — must be live on a public URL at submission

Background

Every monsoon, upazila-level relief coordination in Bangladesh runs on phone calls and paper lists. When a flood hits, the Deputy Commissioner's office receives shelter status by SMS from field volunteers, aid inventory arrives as Excel sheets from NGOs, and nobody has a single picture of where people are and what they need. Decisions that should take minutes take hours.

Build Relief Lens: a coordination dashboard a DC office could actually use during a flood — shelters on a map, live capacity, incoming aid stock, and a matching view that shows which shelters are critically short of which supplies.

MVP requirements (pass/fail — all judged on the live URL)

1. Shelter map with live status. An interactive map of the district showing every shelter as a marker, colour-coded by occupancy level (OK / near-full / over capacity). Clicking a marker shows the shelter's name, current headcount, capacity, and its three most-needed supply items. The app must load with realistic seeded data — a judge opening the URL cold must see a believable district in mid-flood, not an empty map.
2. Field update entry. A volunteer-facing form to submit a status update for a shelter: headcount, urgent needs (from a fixed supply list), and a free-text note. Submitting must update the map marker's colour and popup without a page reload, and the update must appear in a visible "recent updates" feed with a relative timestamp ("2 min ago").
3. Aid inventory import. Upload a CSV of incoming aid consignments (columns: ngo_name, item, quantity, unit, eta_hours) — the app must parse it, reject malformed rows with a visible per-row error report (not a silent drop and not a crash), and merge valid rows into a searchable, filterable inventory table. A sample CSV must be downloadable from the app itself so the judge can test the flow.
4. Shortage matching board. A view that crosses shelter needs against imported inventory and ranks shelters by severity: for each critically short item, show which shelters need it, how much stock exists across all consignments, and the gap. Severity must be visibly explained (the judge should understand why shelter X outranks shelter Y without asking), and the board must update when either a field update or a CSV import changes the underlying data.

Notes from the organisers

  • Seeded demo data is not optional — an empty app fails bullet 1.
  • "Live" means the deployed public URL. Localhost demos are not judged.
  • Any data source may be fictional, but units and geography should be plausible for a Bangladeshi district (BDT, bags/kg/litres, real upazila names welcome).

Submission (at the 2-hour mark — hard stop, mirrors the real event)

  1. Live public URL — the deployed app, working. Localhost scores zero.
  2. Repo — pushed, with README.md (what it is, how to run) and LICENSES.md (every dependency, its licence).
  3. 60-second demo video — walk the four bullets on the live URL, in order. Record it inside the last 15 minutes; follow your acceptance script.

Stretch (unscored in this drill — only after 4/4)

  • Bengali/English label toggle
  • Shelter-level trend sparkline (headcount over time)
  • Printable one-page situation report
